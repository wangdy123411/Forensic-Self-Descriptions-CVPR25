# Research and implementation audit

This audit records the sources inspected on 8 November 2026. Repository availability and dataset packaging describe that inspection, rather than a continuing status check. The [completed results](../RESULTS.md) report measurements from the AN study.

## Repository and scope

The local Git remote is `https://github.com/wangdy123411/Forensic-Self-Descriptions-CVPR25.git`. Its README and package metadata identify [ductai199x/Forensic-Self-Descriptions-CVPR25](https://github.com/ductai199x/Forensic-Self-Descriptions-CVPR25) as the official implementation. The inspected local revision is `50f2eae06efdac2e5a33f407ca9a27a2295133ac`, dated 15 March 2026; its commit message identifies v1.2.0. Keep the exact code revision, downloaded weight hashes, and runtime configuration with each experiment.

The cited work is Nguyen, Azizpour, and Stamm, **Forensic Self-Descriptions Are All You Need for Zero-Shot Detection, Open-Set Source Attribution, and Clustering of AI-generated Images**, CVPR 2025. The [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21003v1) includes its supplementary appendices. The direct CVF URLs were intermittently inaccessible during this audit, so the accessible author manuscript was used alongside the [CVPR publication record](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Nguyen_Forensic_Self-Descriptions_Are_All_You_Need_for_Zero-Shot_Detection_Open-Set_CVPR_2025_paper.html).

AN's slide describes a robustness study: transform the same held-out images, measure changes in residuals and 960-dimensional descriptors, and connect those changes to task performance. It does not require retraining the entire model. The practical target is therefore a reproducible audit of the released detector under image processing, with explicit separation from a full paper reproduction.

## Original experimental data and protocol

The paper's Appendix A gives the following **paper-specific counts**, rather than the sizes of the entire public datasets:

| Real source | Training | Testing |
|---|---:|---:|

| ImageNet-1k | 100,000 | 1,000 |
| ImageNet-22k | 100,000 | 1,000 |
| MISL Image Database | 22,329 | 1,000 |

Synthetic evaluation pools OSSIA, DMID, Synthbuster, and author-generated images: 24 generators, 29,000 test images. Synthetic training counts belong to attribution/comparisons, not zero-shot detection. Known attribution sources are ImageNet-1k, StyleGAN, StyleGAN3, SD1.4, ProGAN; unknowns are StyleGAN2, SD3, DALL-E3, Firefly. Appendix A does not specify a separate validation count or publish exact sample identifiers. The paper uses eight 11×11 residual filters, three scales, and per-image AdamW optimization up to 10,000 iterations. Its JPEG study covers Q100/90/80/70/60/50 on ImageNet-1k versus ProGAN/SDXL/DALL-E3/MJv6/Firefly: clean AUROC .986, processed .960–.979. The source of these counts and settings is [the paper, Sections 5–7 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21003v1).

**Reproduction gap:** identifying a public dataset does not identify the paper's exact subset. Do not claim the paper's train/test disjointness for a newly downloaded subset without manifests or hashes. The released weights also do not provide image-level training provenance in the inspected repository. A held-out split made locally guarantees separation inside this experiment, but cannot establish separation from every image used by the checkpoint authors.

## Dataset access and suitable choices

| Source | Official access and contents | Implication for this run |
|---|---|---|
| COCO2017 | [COCO download page](https://cocodataset.org/#download); official photos and annotations | Useful accessible real-image domain; retain original files and original split IDs. Existing JPEG histories must be acknowledged. |
| ImageNet-1k/22k | [ImageNet access](https://www.image-net.org/download.php) | Do not silently substitute a third-party resized copy for the original benchmark. Access and sample provenance can be limiting. |
| MISL Image Database | FSD Appendix A cites the camera-forensics collections underlying MIDB; [MISL lab](https://misl.ece.drexel.edu/) | Exact FSD image list is not present in the inspected release. Treat reconstruction as unresolved rather than assuming a matching collection. |
| OSSIA | [Author-hosted Hugging Face dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ductai199x/open-set-synth-img-attribution) | Official data card: 1,147,422 images, split 803,195 train / 114,742 validation / 229,485 test; approximately 424 GB in repository files. Download a defined test subset rather than the entire collection. |
| DMID | [Corvi et al. implementation/data](https://github.com/grip-unina/DMimageDetection) | Synthetic test download includes a CSV specifying processing; real images are sourced separately from ImageNet, UCID, and COCO. Preserve CSV provenance and distinguish pristine from preprocessed samples. |
| Synthbuster | [Bammey's dataset record](https://zenodo.org/records/10066460) | 9,000 images, 1,000 per generator, 12.4 GB ZIP. The curator reports no added JPEG compression or resampling. This does not imply lossless provider output: the selected Firefly members have JPEG payloads despite their .png filenames. Native encoding is recorded before applying any new processing. |
| RAISE-1k | [Original RAISE access](http://loki.disi.unitn.it/RAISE/download.html); [GRIP author-maintained download script](https://github.com/grip-unina/ClipBased-SyntheticImageDetection/blob/main/data/synthbuster_download.sh) | Synthbuster recommends RAISE-1k as its real counterpart. This is an additional real domain, not a reproduction of FSD's four real domains. |

OSSIA covers ProGAN, Projected-GAN, StyleGAN, StyleGAN2, StyleGAN3, Taming Transformer, and Stable Diffusion; its authors deliberately preserve original resolutions and provide generator/architecture labels. Its data card says the packaging is already shuffled and supplies class mappings. This is useful for GAN coverage and split-aware attribution. [OSSIA dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ductai199x/open-set-synth-img-attribution)

Synthbuster contains DALL-E2, DALL-E3, Adobe Firefly, Midjourney v5, Stable Diffusion 1.3, 1.4, 2, XL, and GLIDE. Its prompts were derived from descriptions of RAISE photographs, with manual edits; this gives related semantic content, not pixel-level image pairs. The `prompts.csv` records prompts and parameters. Its files are therefore attractive for controlled JPEG/resize/blur comparisons, but effects should be reported per generator. [Synthbuster dataset record](https://zenodo.org/records/10066460)

The completed study uses balanced, seeded subsets from four Synthbuster sources and COCO, with the same originals under every condition. This choice supports a controlled within-image comparison at manageable compute cost, but limits claims about other real-image domains and generators. The exact selection is documented in [dataset_access.md](dataset_access.md).

## Released implementation audit

The observations below come from the actual local source files. They are especially relevant when interpreting differences from a published number.

| Component | Inspected behavior | Consequence for AN |
|---|---|---|
| `fsd/fre.py` | Default constructor is 8 residual channels with a 15×15 constrained convolution. Loading infers the actual dimensions from checkpoint tensors. The downloaded v1.2.0 configuration also specifies 8 channels and 15×15. Inputs are grayscale values 0–255. | Record the loaded tensor shape as the final check. The actual released configuration differs from the paper's residual filter size. |
| `fsd/fsd_computation.py` | Forms normal equations in float64 and solves a KKT system with one sum-to-one constraint per residual channel and ridge `1e-5`. | This is a released-code evaluation; changing precision or solver requires explicit numerical validation. |
| Scale handling | Concatenates observations from each bilinearly downsampled residual scale into the accumulated normal equations. | Changing the number of scales changes the fitted feature distribution even though its output dimension remains 960. |
| Image normalization | The `resize_and_crop` path rescales the **residual tensor**, setting its shorter dimension to `max_size`, then center crops; it can upsample a small input. | Input downsampling can be followed by internal upsampling. Report both operations, and do not interpret this as a native-resolution-only test. |
| Memory | Each scale's unfolded patches are formed before chunked accumulation of `XTX`. | Chunking the accumulation alone does not eliminate the full patch allocation. The AN tiled and FFT alternatives were checked against the original calculation; the direct comparison is documented separately. |
| `fsd/projection.py` | Applies one or more learned residual MLP transforms to descriptors before density scoring; additional attribution transforms are supported. | Keep raw 960D descriptor drift separate from drift after learned transforms. Checkpoint transformations can amplify small changes. |
| `fsd/detector.py` | Computes a real-density log likelihood and normalizes it using stored train mean/std; lower z means more synthetic. `score_batch` loops over images. | Use `-z` or negative log likelihood as the positive/fake AUROC score. A nominal batch API is not batched feature extraction. |
| `fsd/attribution.py` | Picks the largest per-source log likelihood and applies softmax temperature 750 to produce confidences. There is no unknown-source rejection branch in `classify`. | Describe this as released closed-set source prediction. The confidence is not an externally validated probability of correctness. |
| API attribution output | `scores` is a probability dictionary, despite some README wording describing likelihoods. | Compute and preserve raw likelihoods separately if studying rejection thresholds. |
| `fsd/weights.py` | Pins automatic downloads to release v1.2.0 and normally writes to a home-directory cache. | Supply `an/weights/...` explicitly to keep the task self-contained. Hash downloads and preserve `config.json`. |
| Training release | The inspected tree contains inference and GMM routines, but no complete FRE/feature-transform training pipeline with original data manifests. | A full retraining reproduction is outside the available implementation. Preserve the checkpoint's learned functions for robustness inference. |

The paper-to-code differences mean that matching a paper score cannot be assumed from sharing the name FSD. In particular, the release's feature transforms and inference implementation need their own provenance. The AN extraction optimization preserves the released closed-form objective; it does not reproduce the paper's iterative optimization or training procedure. The upstream README identifies detection and attribution releases in 2026 and still lists clustering code as forthcoming. [Official repository](https://github.com/ductai199x/Forensic-Self-Descriptions-CVPR25)

## Design implications

The implementation audit motivates three controls. First, native image bytes and
source metadata must be retained because format history can differ across
sources. Second, clean and processed images must share the same fixed model,
thresholds, and score direction. Third, descriptor drift should be separated
from task performance: a large feature change is descriptive evidence, not proof
of a particular failure mechanism.

The completed [experiment protocol](experiment_plan.md) applies these controls to
19 paired conditions, with source-stratified uncertainty over original images.
It reports ranking and operating-point metrics together, and preserves raw
features for analysis without repeating extraction. The
[implementation comparison](../baseline_comparison/RESULTS.md) then checks the
optimized extractor against the authors' original code on identical inputs.

Potential improvements require separate development data and fresh evaluation.
Processing-aware calibration can address operating-point shifts when ranking
survives. Native-resolution crops or crop aggregation may address sensitivity to
internal resizing, but alter feature distributions and compute cost. Neither
approach was validated as a mitigation in this study.

The released classifier's closed-set source predictions should not be interpreted
as open-set attribution. A rejection experiment would need whole held-out
generators, separate calibration, and measures of both known-source accuracy and
unknown rejection. Likewise, clustering after a source-supervised feature
transform differs from an unsupervised representation claim. These extensions,
full retraining, and the complete 96-pair paper benchmark are outside the present
AN evaluation.
